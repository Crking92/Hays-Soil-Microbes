What am I looking at?

This page translates soil and microbial data into practical land-care ideas. It does not tell you exactly what microbes are in your yard. Instead, it shows what the soil conditions are likely to support and what benefits regional microbial evidence suggests.

  1. Search your soil or habitat. Look for soil names, texture, clay, limestone, drainage, or habitat words.
  2. Read the benefit cards. They explain why soil microbes matter for gardens and restoration.
  3. Use this for planning. Match plants and management to soil conditions instead of fighting the soil.
  4. Confirm locally when needed. For exact decisions, collect a soil test or local sample.

Find a soil or habitat

What the microbial evidence means

Simple version: regional data show microbial potential for cycling nutrients, decomposing organic matter, handling stress, and supporting soil structure. This is a clue, not a yard-by-yard test result.

Top benefit signals

Practical takeaways

Do not over-amend first

Start by learning the soil texture, drainage, and pH. Many native plants prefer the soil as it is.

Keep roots in the ground

Living roots feed soil microbes. Native perennials, grasses, and diverse plantings help maintain soil life.

Protect organic matter

Mulch, leaf litter, compost used carefully, and reduced disturbance can support decomposition and nutrient cycling.

Match plants to place

Shallow limestone soils, deeper clays, and riparian soils support different plant communities and management strategies.
