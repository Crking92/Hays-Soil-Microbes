Six tiny soil-worker jobs

Beginners do not need to memorize Latin names or gene codes. Start with what the organisms do.

1. Recyclers

Break down dead leaves, roots, mulch, and compost.

Like: tiny cleanup crews turning leftovers into soil food.

2. Nutrient unlockers

Help move nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus, sulfur, iron, and minerals into usable forms.

Like: opening locked food boxes for plants.

3. Root helpers

Live near roots and help with nutrient exchange, root growth, and soil structure.

Like: neighbors around the root grocery store.

4. Soil builders

Help glue soil into crumbs so air and water can move.

Like: builders making the soil into a sponge.

5. Drought survivors

Survive heat, drying, and wet-dry swings common in Central Texas.

Like: tiny workers that can pause and restart after rain.

6. Balance keepers

Change with clay, limestone, water, oxygen, roots, and organic matter.

Like: different teams showing up for different soil conditions.

The soil food web in plain language

The soil food web is the living network belowground. Plants feed microbes. Microbes recycle organic matter. Tiny predators release nutrients. Roots and fungi help build structure.

Why are there numbers here?

The numbers are counts of regional gene/function signals from processed microbial data. A bigger number usually means that signal appeared more often in this dataset. It does not mean the function is active in your exact yard.
