Why we think this

The beginner promise

No jargon first

Beginners see soil personalities, root meaning, first actions, and avoid-for-now warnings before technical tables.

Soil-life jobs

Microbial data are translated into jobs: recyclers, nutrient unlockers, root helpers, soil builders, drought survivors, and balance keepers.

Backyard tests

Simple tests let users compare the soil map to what they see: water soak-in, ribbon texture, and shovel/root-zone checks.

Honest evidence

The dashboard explains what is mapped, what is inferred, and what requires a real local soil or microbe test.

What the Soil Habitat Translator can tell you

Soil setting

Whether a site is likely clayey, limestone-influenced, shallow, wet, droughty, loamy, prairie-like, or woodland-like.

Plant meaning

How the soil may affect roots, water, nutrient access, compaction, and native plant matching.

Microbe jobs

Which soil-life functions may be favored: recycling, nutrient unlocking, root-zone support, soil building, drought survival, or wet-soil processing.

Stewardship actions

What practices usually help: living roots, soil cover, less disturbance, soil-matched natives, and careful fertilizer decisions.

What this dashboard cannot prove by itself

Safe public wording

  • This soil may create habitat for microbes that do these jobs.
  • Mapped soil properties suggest likely soil-life functions.
  • Regional microbial data provide comparison evidence, not exact-site proof.

Avoid overclaiming

  • Do not say the dashboard proves a specific microbe is present in one yard.
  • Do not say genes prove active nutrient cycling right now.
  • Do not use this as a fertilizer prescription or engineering determination.
