The evidence story in three layers

The dashboard is strongest when it explains soil habitat: clay, limestone, water, air, roots, and organic matter. Microbe names and genes are useful evidence, but they are not the first public story.

Local soil layer Soil maps and lab pedons describe texture, clay, pH, carbonate, drainage, depth, and organic matter.
Regional microbe layer Regional metagenome datasets show microbial names and gene-function signals observed in similar Texas soil data.
Prediction layer Habitat rules translate soil clues into likely soil-life jobs. These are predictions, not direct site tests.
Public claim layer Use evidence grades before saying something is local, confirmed, regional, or predicted.

Mapped soils, soil properties, and local soil-habitat summaries.

Microbial taxa and gene-function annotations from regional analog datasets.

What is inferred?

The link between soil type and likely soil-life jobs.

Exact microbe names, exact plant-root partners, and exact activity at one property.

      How to read the science

      1. Soil is the habitat

      Clay, limestone, drainage, depth, pH, and organic matter shape what roots and microbes can do.

      2. Roots feed the system

      Living roots leak sugars and other compounds. That root food supports bacteria, fungi, and other soil organisms.

      3. Microbes have jobs

      The useful public story is not memorizing names. It is understanding jobs: recycling, unlocking nutrients, building structure, and surviving stress.

      4. Evidence has levels

      Mapped soil evidence is not the same as local DNA evidence. The dashboard labels that difference so claims stay honest.
